# Tech & innovation fluency

Write like someone who actually works in tech. Use terms correctly, know the
audience, and never let a buzzword stand in for a real claim.

## Map of the tech universe (know enough to be credible)
- **AI / ML**: LLMs, tokens/context windows, embeddings, RAG, fine-tuning vs.
  prompting, agents, inference cost/latency, evals, hallucination. Don't call
  everything "AI"; be specific about what the model does.
- **Cloud & infra**: containers, Kubernetes, serverless, IaC, CI/CD, observability,
  multi-cloud/cloud-agnostic, edge, cost/FinOps.
- **SaaS & product**: onboarding, activation, PLG, churn, ARR/MRR, integrations,
  multi-tenancy, SSO, usage-based pricing.
- **Dev tools & DX**: SDKs, APIs, DX, time-to-first-call, docs-as-product, CLIs.
- **Data**: warehouses/lakehouses, ETL/ELT, streaming, analytics, governance.
- **Security & privacy**: zero trust, secrets, SOC 2, LGPD/GDPR, RLS, supply chain.
- **Startups/VC**: MVP, PMF, moat, GTM, seed→Series, positioning.

## Get it right
- Precision beats hype. "Sub-100ms p95 on cached reads" > "blazing fast".
- Name real tools/standards when relevant (Postgres, OAuth, OpenTelemetry) — it
  signals you know the space.
- Explain a concept **only** when the audience needs it; respect experts' time.
- **Never** invent benchmarks, model names, versions, or capabilities. If unsure,
  verify or omit.

## Audience-fit
- Developers value honesty about tradeoffs and limitations more than superlatives.
- Buyers/execs want outcome, risk, and cost. PMs want workflow fit and proof.

## Currency
- Tech moves fast — prefer durable claims; date anything time-sensitive; avoid
  "the latest" without a version/date. When trends matter (AI search, agents,
  edge), reference them accurately, not as decoration.

## Trust signals for a tech audience
- Concrete numbers, real architecture details, honest limitations, named
  customers/logos (if permitted), and links to docs/benchmarks. Skepticism is the
  default in tech — earn belief with specifics.
