# Copy patterns (repeatable moves that work)

## Openings
- **Cold open with a specific**: a number, a scene, or the reader's exact problem.
  ("Your build takes 12 minutes. Here's how we got ours to 40 seconds.")
- **Answer-first**: state the takeaway in sentence one; earn the rest.

## Making claims believable
- **Claim → proof** pairs: never a benefit without a mechanism, number, or example
  right next to it.
- **Show the tradeoff**: naming a limitation increases trust with tech readers.
- **Before/after with real metrics** (verified).

## Structure
- **One idea per section**, subhead states the idea. A skimmer reading only
  subheads should still get the argument.
- **Rule of one**: one reader, one problem, one promise, one CTA.
- Break patterns on purpose: a one-line paragraph after a dense one lands harder.

## Word-level
- Cut the first draft's first paragraph (usually warm-up).
- Replace abstractions with the concrete noun/number.
- Prefer verbs to nominalizations ("decide" > "make a decision").
- Trim 10–20% in the edit — tighten every sentence.

## CTAs
- Phrase as value, not action-label ("See the benchmark" > "Learn more").
- Reduce risk right at the CTA (free, no card, 2-minute setup).

## Editing loop
1. Draft fast (don't self-edit while drafting).
2. Cut filler + AI tells (`anti-patterns.md`).
3. Add specifics/proof where claims are vague.
4. Read aloud; fix anything that trips.
5. SEO pass (title, H1, intent, meta) — without hurting clarity.
