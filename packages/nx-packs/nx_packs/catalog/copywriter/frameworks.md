# Copy frameworks (structure that persuades)

Frameworks are scaffolding, not straitjackets. Pick one, then make it sound human.

## Message architecture
- **AIDA** — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Good for landing pages/ads.
- **PAS** — Problem → Agitate → Solve. Great when the pain is felt (dev pain,
  ops toil). Name the problem precisely; don't over-agitate.
- **BAB** — Before → After → Bridge. Show the new reality, then how to get there.
- **FAB** — Feature → Advantage → Benefit. Always land on the reader's benefit,
  not the spec.
- **4 Ps** — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push (CTA).

## Headlines
- Lead with the outcome or the tension. Specific > clever. Numbers and concrete
  nouns win ("Deploy in 90 seconds" > "Deployment, reimagined").
- Formulas that work: outcome ("Ship X without Y"), how-to ("How we cut Z"),
  question the reader is asking, contrarian take (if true).
- Write 10, keep 1. Cut adjectives.

## Openings (the hook)
- Earn the second sentence: a sharp claim, a specific scene, a real number, or the
  reader's exact problem. **No warm-up paragraph.**

## Structure
- One idea per section; descriptive subheads that a skimmer could read alone.
- Proof near every claim (benchmark, example, quote, screenshot).
- End with **one** CTA, phrased as the value ("Start free", "See the benchmark"),
  not "Submit".

## CTAs
- One primary action per page; make it specific and low-friction. Reduce risk
  (free, no card, 2-min setup). Match the CTA to the awareness stage.

## Formats
- **Landing/hero**: outcome headline, subhead with the mechanism, 3 proof points,
  CTA. **Blog**: answer-first intro, scannable sections, one takeaway, soft CTA.
  **Product copy**: benefit-led, feature-backed, objection-handling. **Email**:
  one idea, one CTA, subject that earns the open.
