# Voice & tone — sounding like a real person

**Voice** is constant (who the brand is). **Tone** flexes with context (a pricing
page vs. an incident post-mortem). Default for tech/innovation: **confident,
clear, specific, a little opinionated — never hypey.**

## Techniques that read human
- **Vary rhythm.** Follow a long sentence with a short one. Fragments are fine.
- **Active voice + strong verbs.** "We shipped it" beats "It was shipped."
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Cut cold-start from 900ms to 120ms," not "improved
  performance significantly."
- **One point of view.** Say what you actually think; take a side.
- **Plain words.** "Use", not "utilize". "Help", not "facilitate".
- **Contractions** where natural (don't, you'll). Second person ("you") pulls the
  reader in.
- **Specific nouns.** "Postgres", "p95 latency", "GPT-4-class model" — not "the
  technology", "the solution".

## Calibrate to the audience
- **Developers**: precise, no fluff, show the tradeoff, respect their time.
- **Founders/execs**: outcomes, speed, risk, cost — lead with the "so what".
- **PMs/buyers**: how it fits the workflow, proof, and what it replaces.
- Match **awareness stage**: problem-aware readers need the pain named; product-
  aware readers need differentiation and proof.

## Register
- Professional ≠ stiff. Warm ≠ cutesy. Aim for "smart colleague explaining a real
  thing," not "brand shouting benefits."
- Humor sparingly and only if it fits the brand; never at the cost of clarity.

## Language
- Write natively in the target language (PT-BR / EN) — **translate ideas, not
  words.** Localize idioms, examples, and rhythm; don't calque.

## Consistency
- Keep a short style sheet per brand (terms, capitalization, do/don't words, CTA
  phrasing) so every piece sounds like the same person.
