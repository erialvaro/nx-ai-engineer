# Anti-patterns — the AI tells & clichés (never write these)

The fastest way to sound like a bot. Screen every draft against this list.

## Opening / throat-clearing
- "In today's fast-paced world…", "In the ever-evolving landscape of…",
  "In the digital age…", "Now more than ever…".
- "In this article, we'll explore…" / "Let's dive in." — just start.
- Restating the prompt/title as the first sentence.

## Filler transitions & hedging
- Overused: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion",
  "It's worth noting that", "It's important to note", "That said".
- Empty hedges: "can help to", "may potentially", "arguably", "in many ways",
  "when it comes to…".

## AI-favorite vocabulary (use sparingly / prefer plain words)
- "delve", "leverage" (→ use), "utilize" (→ use), "robust", "seamless",
  "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "revolutionary", "unlock", "elevate",
  "supercharge", "empower", "streamline", "harness", "navigate the complexities",
  "tapestry", "realm", "testament to", "at the end of the day".
- Adjective stacking: "powerful, intuitive, seamless, next-generation platform".

## Structural tics
- Every section the same length; every list exactly three items; a bullet list
  for everything (some ideas are paragraphs).
- The "It's not just X, it's Y" formula, repeated.
- Over-symmetry / relentless parallelism; a neat "In conclusion" that summarizes
  what was just said.
- Em-dash overuse as a default connector.

## Voice failures
- No point of view; everything balanced and hedged into blandness.
- Corporate press-release tone; explaining the obvious to experts (or the reverse).
- Emoji-as-bullets in professional copy; exclamation overload.

## Substance failures (worse than style)
- **Fabricated** stats, benchmarks, quotes, or capabilities.
- Buzzword salad with no concrete claim ("AI-powered synergy at scale").
- Vague benefits ("boost productivity") with no mechanism or proof.
- Keyword stuffing / writing for robots over humans.

## The fix (what humans do instead)
Vary rhythm. Make a claim and back it with a specific. Have an opinion. Use plain
verbs. Cut the first paragraph if it's warm-up. Read it aloud — if a sentence
wouldn't survive being said to a smart colleague, rewrite it.
